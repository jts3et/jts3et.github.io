Enacting Likeness: Metaphor and the Acquisition of Tacit Knowledge

Abstraction and Analogy Group, Diverse Intelligences Summer Institute | June 2026–present

Led a five-person group (with researchers at the University of Texas at Austin, the University of Pittsburgh, Macquarie University, and the University of California, Merced) modeling metaphorical instruction as a constraint on bodily coordination: the teaching image enters a Gaussian graphical model as one component of a single precision matrix — baseline stiffness, anatomical structure, metaphorical constraint — restricting the space of possible coordinations rather than transferring content, with relative phase as the collective order parameter. On Taijiquan motion capture (twelve performers), bilateral arm phase-locking tracks judged skill (Spearman ρ = +0.68); synthetic and CMU motion-capture analyses show convergence under shared metaphors, task-dependent sign flips in joint coupling, and frustration under conflicting images. Since the Institute, the group has met weekly to design a controlled study isolating what an instructional image contributes; a conceptual and modeling paper is in preparation for Philosophy and the Mind Sciences, and a Big ECCE Award application proposes a twenty-participant pilot. Methodology; companion.

How to Read a Poem — Guided-Reading Workshop and Tool

Diverse Intelligences Summer Institute | June–July 2026

Interactive workshop site and guided-reading tool built for the Diverse Intelligences Summer Institute: live collective scansion exercises whose disagreements land on a shared board; versification instruction covering meter, form, and genre from Homer to free verse; meter as the shape of the breath; and a collaborative full reading of The Waste Land. The Discernment Research Collaborative’s Templeton proposal, “Renewing Common Culture” (under review), adapts the tool as its intervention. Site.

Metamodality Reading Group — “An Inquiry into Forms of the Possible”

Center for Possible Minds, Indiana University Bloomington | Spring 2026

Curriculum traces the concept of the possible across philosophical traditions, from Parmenides and Aristotle through Avicenna, Leibniz, Kant, and Whitehead to contemporary modal logic. Site.
