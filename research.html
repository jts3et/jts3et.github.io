Research

Continental philosophy · philosophy of literature · poetry and poetics · narratology · philosophy of mind

An impersonal theory of emotion organizes the current papers, emotion understood through its conditioning structures rather than through the subject who has it. The terms come from T. S. Eliot’s early philosophical writing, the Harvard dissertation and the graduate essays around it, which I read as a bridge between British Idealism and Husserlian phenomenology; I put those terms to individuation and emotion, to subsistence and objectivity, and to the history of Eliot’s own verse. I am developing the account into a book, Objective Perspectival Realism: Individuation, Objectivity, and Impersonality after T. S. Eliot.
