Computational work runs alongside the papers. The projects below range from a Gaussian graphical model of instructional metaphor to a guided-reading tool. Most leave a public instrument behind, a site, a board, a methodology page; links follow each description.

“The Automation of Critical Analysis”

Scaling Mathematical Folklore, Division of Applied Mathematics, Brown University (Govind Menon) · Fall 2026

Mentor to a team of seniors in “The Automation of Critical Analysis,” a focus area of Govind Menon’s independent study “Scaling Mathematical Folklore,” in which students read Derrida and Eliot with the help of AI tools to ask, in Menon’s words, “What exactly defines taste?”

Enacting Likeness: Metaphor and the Acquisition of Tacit Knowledge

Abstraction and Analogy Group, Diverse Intelligences Summer Institute · June 2026–present

A five-person group, which I led, with researchers at the University of Texas at Austin, the University of Pittsburgh, Macquarie University, and the University of California, Merced. The teaching image enters a Gaussian graphical model as one component of a single precision matrix, alongside baseline stiffness and anatomical structure; it restricts the space of possible coordinations and transfers no content. On Taijiquan motion capture from twelve performers, bilateral arm phase-locking tracks judged skill, and synthetic and CMU analyses find coordinations converging under shared metaphors and frustrated under conflicting ones. Since the Institute the group has met weekly to design a controlled study isolating what an instructional image contributes; a conceptual and modeling paper is in preparation, and a Big ECCE Award application proposes a twenty-participant pilot.

How to Read a Poem

Guided-reading workshop and tool, Diverse Intelligences Summer Institute · 2026

An interactive workshop site and guided-reading tool. Live collective scansion exercises land their disagreements on a shared board, versification instruction covers meter, form, and genre from Homer to free verse, and the workshop closes on a collaborative full reading of The Waste Land. The Discernment Research Collaborative’s Templeton proposal, “Renewing Common Culture” (under review), adapts the tool as its intervention.
