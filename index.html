Justin Thomas Stec

Postdoctoral Research Associate, Center for Possible Minds
Indiana University Bloomington

I work as a Continental philosopher of aesthetics and emotion at the Center for Possible Minds, Indiana University Bloomington, a Templeton-funded initiative studying intelligence in biological, artificial, and collective forms. My research combines philosophy of mind and cognitive science with the metaphysics of possibility and the phenomenological, formal, and computational study of poetry.

The program began as a dissertation. “Cognitive-Affective Formalism: T. S. Eliot and the Embodiment of Early Modern Verse” (University of Virginia, 2024), directed by Stephen Cushman, reads Eliot’s essays on early modern verse as a phenomenology of embodied reading. Before the doctorate I trained in two subjects at once, biochemistry and English; the pairing has held. Close reading, philosophical argument, and computational modeling still run together in everything I make.

Since the computational social scientist Jacob Gates Foster and the cognitive scientist Erica Cartmill launched the Center in fall 2025, I have helped build it from the ground up, shaping its programming, computational infrastructure, and network of affiliated scholars across five departments. I led the Abstraction and Analogy Group at the Diverse Intelligences Summer Institute, a five-person group modeling metaphorical instruction as a constraint on bodily coordination; since the Institute we have met weekly to design a controlled study of what an instructional image contributes. This fall I mentor a team of Brown seniors in “The Automation of Critical Analysis,” a focus area of Govind Menon’s independent study “Scaling Mathematical Folklore,” in which students read Derrida and Eliot with the help of AI tools. The projects page gathers the computational work, with its public sites and code.
